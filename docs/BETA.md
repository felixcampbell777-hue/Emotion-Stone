## Existing human motion before manual authoring

Research examined [Quaternius Universal Animation Library](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html), its [free Standard and paid source options](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-animation-library), [Library 2](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary2.html), [CMU motion capture](https://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/) and [Godot's rest-aware retargeting documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html). Quaternius offers broad reusable locomotion/combat sets; CMU offers real movement but requires cleanup and rig matching. No purchase or additional animation download was needed for this small prototype. Keep the paid Quaternius source-library link above as a later option, rather than buying an overlapping movement set now.

The selected first strategy reuses the nineteen existing project movement/combat actions, preserving the sword clocks and hit trajectories. tools/beta_rig.py consolidates the saved human weights onto sixteen deform bones; Godot rest-relative rotation adaptation drives that skin from the canonical actor rig. Beta keeps that hidden motion rig on Original regardless of the world graphics profile, and renders the personal baked skin; it does not use the older fitted Advanced motion rig. The hidden canonical body continues to own input, combat and animation sampling; only the personal model and live equipment are rendered. Inventory uses the same Beta presentation. Embedded animation players do not create competing clocks. This is an initial retargeting pass; hand-to-sword alignment, finger grips and clothing deformation still warrant art polish.
